# SoCal Playbook

Working knowledge for the `socal` subagent. Read in full at the start of every engagement. Platform specifics drift; treat numbers here as starting benchmarks, verify anything time-sensitive with current research, and date-stamp it in deliverables. Update this file when reality has moved on.

---

## 1. Strategy method

### 1.1 Objective cascade
Never start with content. Start here and make every later choice trace back up the chain.

1. **Business goal**: revenue, margin, market share, retention, hiring, reputation.
2. **Marketing objective**: what marketing must change to hit the goal (awareness, consideration, conversion, loyalty, advocacy).
3. **Social objective**: the specific contribution social makes, stated as a measurable outcome with a date.
4. **KPIs**: one primary, two or three secondary, with baselines and targets. See section 6.

If the client cannot state 1 and 2, help them do it before anything else.

### 1.2 Hypothesis-led work
Form a view early ("the channel is under-performing because format mix is 80% static in a Reels-first feed") and then go looking for evidence that confirms or kills it. Write the storyline before the analysis; adjust the storyline when the analysis disagrees. Never boil the ocean.

### 1.3 Structure and communication
- **Pyramid Principle**: answer first, then the grouped supporting arguments, then the evidence.
- **SCQA** for framing: Situation, Complication, Question, Answer.
- **MECE**: every list of drivers, options or pillars is mutually exclusive and collectively exhaustive.
- **Issue tree**: break the core question into sub-questions until each can be answered with data.
- **The "so what" test**: every slide headline states a conclusion or an action, never a topic.
- **80/20**: find the two or three moves that deliver most of the value and lead with them.

### 1.4 Diagnostic toolkit
- **Channel audit**: for each channel, record follower base, 90-day reach, engagement rate by reach, format mix, cadence actually achieved vs. planned, top and bottom 10 posts and why, response time, profile completeness, link and shopping setup.
- **Competitor and category benchmark**: 3 to 5 direct competitors plus 2 best-in-class from any category. Compare cadence, format mix, engagement rate, share of voice, content themes, creator use, community tone. Identify white space the client can own.
- **Audience insight**: platform analytics (age, location, active hours), first-party data (CRM, customer research), social listening (what they say, ask, complain about), search and hashtag demand. Write 2 to 4 audience segments as people with jobs-to-be-done, not demographics alone.
- **Content gap analysis**: map competitor and category content against audience needs; the gaps are the opportunity.
- **SWOT** only when it changes a decision; otherwise skip it.

### 1.5 Channel role model
Give every channel one primary job, or cut it. Common roles: reach and discovery (TikTok, Reels, Shorts), depth and trust (YouTube long-form, LinkedIn, newsletters), conversion and shopping (Instagram, Facebook, Pinterest), service and community (Facebook groups, X, DMs, broadcast channels), employer brand (LinkedIn). Fewer channels done well beats more channels done adequately. State explicitly which channels to stop or park.

### 1.6 Content strategy design
- **Content pillars**: 3 to 5 themes that sit at the intersection of what the audience wants and what the brand can credibly say. Each pillar has a purpose, an audience need, example formats and a share of the calendar.
- **Content mix**: a working default is 60 to 70% value (educate, entertain, inspire), 20 to 30% brand and community (behind the scenes, people, UGC, conversation), 10 to 20% direct sell. Adjust to the objective.
- **Hero, hub, help**: hero moments for tentpoles and launches, hub content that keeps regulars coming back, help content that answers what people search for.
- **Messaging house**: one brand promise, three to four proof pillars, supporting messages per audience segment.
- **Creative principles**: 4 to 6 short rules that make the brand recognisable in feed without the logo (visual signature, first-frame device, sonic cue, caption voice).

### 1.7 Operating model
Define who does what: strategy, planning, creative production, copy, community, paid, reporting. Set the workflow (brief, draft, review, approve, schedule, publish, respond, report), approval SLAs, tooling (scheduler, asset library, listening, reporting), and a rule for reactive content (who can approve within one hour). Under-resourced plans fail; size the plan to the team.

### 1.8 Roadmap
Sequence in phases: fix the foundations (profiles, pillars, cadence, measurement), build the engine (production system, formats, creators), scale (paid amplification, new channels, partnerships). Show quick wins in the first 30 days. Show dependencies and decision points.

## 2. Platform playbooks

Verify specifics quarterly. Engagement benchmarks are by reach unless stated; by-follower rates are lower and less useful.

### 2.1 Instagram
- **What the algorithm rewards**: watch time and replays on Reels, sends (shares via DM), saves, comments, and early velocity. Sends are the strongest signal for reach beyond followers.
- **Formats**: Reels for reach, carousels for saves and depth (carousels get a second impression if not swiped), Stories for daily relationship and conversion (links, polls, question stickers), broadcast channels for superfans, Lives for launches and Q&A.
- **Cadence baseline**: 3 to 5 feed posts a week with Reels making up half or more; Stories most days; consistency beats volume.
- **Craft**: first frame and first line must stop the scroll; on-screen text for sound-off viewing; 7 to 15 second Reels for reach, 30 to 90 seconds for depth; captions front-load the point, then context, then one call to action; 3 to 5 relevant hashtags at most, keywords in caption and alt text matter more; use trending audio only when it fits the brand.
- **Profile**: name field carries keywords, bio states who it is for and what they get, link in bio goes to a maintained landing page, highlights organised by audience need.
- **Benchmarks**: engagement rate by reach 3 to 6% is healthy; save rate above 1% and share rate above 0.5% of reach indicate strong content.

### 2.2 TikTok
- **What the algorithm rewards**: completion rate, rewatches, shares, comments, and whether viewers who are not followers watch through. Every post is tested with a small audience first; follower count matters less than on any other platform.
- **Craft**: hook in the first 1 to 2 seconds (visual disruption, bold claim, open question, pattern break); deliver on the hook fast; use native text, trending sounds and formats where they fit; face-to-camera and lo-fi often outperform polished; end with a reason to comment or rewatch. Optimal length depends on retention curve, not a fixed number; check the analytics for where viewers drop.
- **Cadence baseline**: 3 to 7 posts a week; more when testing formats.
- **Search**: TikTok is a search engine for under-35s. Put keywords in the spoken audio, on-screen text and caption.
- **Benchmarks**: average watch time above 50% of video length is strong; engagement rate by views 4 to 8% is healthy.

### 2.3 LinkedIn
- **What the algorithm rewards**: dwell time, comments (especially from outside your network), and early engagement in the first hour. Links in the post body reduce reach; put links in the first comment or use a native format.
- **Formats**: text posts with a strong first line (the "see more" cut is the hook), document carousels (PDF) for saves and dwell, native video (short, captioned), polls for reach, articles and newsletters for depth. Personal profiles reach far more than company pages; build an employee and leadership advocacy program.
- **Craft**: one idea per post, short lines, white space, specific numbers and stories, end with a question that invites expertise. Avoid engagement-bait. Post in working hours of the target market, Tuesday to Thursday strongest.
- **Cadence baseline**: company page 3 to 5 a week; executives 2 to 4 a week.
- **Benchmarks**: engagement rate by impressions 2 to 5% for pages, higher for personal profiles.

### 2.4 YouTube and Shorts
- **What the algorithm rewards**: click-through rate on thumbnail and title, then retention and session time. Shorts run on a swipe feed like TikTok and are judged on viewed vs. swiped away.
- **Craft**: title and thumbnail designed together as a promise, open by confirming the promise, use pattern interrupts every 30 to 60 seconds, chapters for navigation, end screens to the next video. Long-form builds trust and search equity; Shorts build discovery and feed long-form.
- **Cadence baseline**: 1 long-form a week is sustainable for most brands; Shorts 3 or more a week.
- **SEO**: keyword research for titles, descriptions and spoken content; YouTube is the second-largest search engine.
- **Benchmarks**: CTR 4 to 10%; average view duration above 40% for long-form.

### 2.5 Facebook
- **Role today**: community groups, local and older audiences, events, Marketplace, and the largest paid inventory. Organic page reach is low; treat the page as a paid and service hub, groups as the organic community engine.
- **Craft**: Reels and native video, questions that prompt conversation, group-first content, events for local activation. Cross-post from Instagram only where the audience overlaps.
- **Cadence baseline**: 3 to 5 a week for pages; daily prompts in active groups.

### 2.6 X
- **Role**: real-time conversation, news, customer service, tech and finance communities, live events. Reach for brands is constrained; success is about voice, speed and participation.
- **Craft**: short, opinionated, conversational; threads for depth; native images and video; reply and quote to join conversations; consistent service response under one hour in business hours.
- **Cadence baseline**: 1 to 3 posts a day plus replies, only if the team can sustain the conversation.

### 2.7 Pinterest
- **Role**: planning and intent, long content half-life (months, not hours), strong for home, food, fashion, beauty, weddings, travel, DIY, B2B templates.
- **Craft**: vertical 2:3 images, text overlay stating the benefit, keyword-rich titles and descriptions, boards organised by search intent, Idea Pins for reach, product pins for shopping, fresh pins over repins.
- **Cadence baseline**: 5 to 15 pins a week, scheduled.

### 2.8 Threads
- **Role**: text-first conversation with Instagram's graph; good for brand voice, community, and testing takes. Reach is currently generous for replies and conversation starters.
- **Craft**: casual, human, question-led; participate in replies; repost to Instagram Stories when a thread lands.

### 2.9 Cross-platform rules
- Native beats cross-posted: adapt aspect ratio, length, hook and caption per platform. Remove platform watermarks.
- Sound-off first: on-screen text and captions everywhere.
- Accessibility: alt text on images, captions on video, high-contrast text, camel-case hashtags, no text-only-in-image information.
- Legal and trust: disclose paid, gifted and affiliate content with platform tools and clear labels (#ad); use licensed music and images; substantiate claims; respect privacy and consent in UGC; follow platform terms.

## 3. Content development

### 3.1 Hook formulas
Bold claim ("Most brands are wasting their Reels budget"); open question ("Why does this post have 2M views?"); curiosity gap ("The one line we changed that doubled saves"); direct address ("If you run a small business, stop doing this"); before/after; contrarian take; number-led ("3 formats that beat carousels"); story in progress ("We almost cancelled the launch"). Test two hooks per idea.

### 3.2 Story structures
Problem, agitate, solve. Before, after, bridge. Hook, context, payoff, CTA. Listicle with a twist in the last item. Behind the scenes with a lesson. Myth vs. fact. Case study: situation, action, result, lesson.

### 3.3 Caption structure
Line 1 is the hook and stands alone before "more". Body gives context or the value in short lines. One call to action, specific (save this, send to someone who, comment your answer, link in bio for). Hashtags and keywords last. Voice consistent with the brand voice guide; read it aloud.

### 3.4 Creative brief template
Objective and KPI; audience and insight; single-minded message; format and platform specs; hook options; must-include and must-avoid; brand and legal requirements; references; deadline and approvals; how it will be measured.

### 3.5 Repurposing matrix
One pillar asset (long video, article, report, event) becomes: 3 to 5 short vertical clips, a carousel of key points, a quote graphic set, a text post or thread, a Story sequence with poll, a newsletter section, a pin. Plan the derivatives at the brief stage, not after.

### 3.6 UGC and creators
Prefer creators whose audience matches the segment over follower count. Brief on the message and the must-haves, not the script. Secure usage rights for paid amplification. Whitelisting or partnership ads outperform brand-handle ads for cold audiences. Track with unique codes or links.

### 3.7 Brand voice checklist
Sounds like one person; uses the brand's words and avoids its banned words; matches the emotional register of the moment; is specific rather than generic; would be recognisable without the logo.

## 4. Content calendar construction

### 4.1 Build order
1. Lock objectives, pillars, channels and cadence.
2. Place tentpoles: launches, sales moments, seasonal and cultural dates relevant to the audience, events, PR.
3. Allocate pillar mix per week to hit the agreed ratios.
4. Assign formats per platform and slot posting windows from analytics.
5. Add series and recurring formats (weekly Q&A, monthly deep-dive) to reduce ideation load.
6. Leave 15 to 20% of slots open for reactive and trend content.
7. Plan production in batches (shoot days, writing sprints) two to four weeks ahead of publish.
8. Add review and approval dates, and a reporting checkpoint at the end of each month.

### 4.2 Calendar spreadsheet columns
ID; publish date; time; platform; format; pillar; campaign or tentpole; hook or headline; caption; visual or video description; asset link; CTA and link; hashtags and keywords; owner; status (idea, drafted, in review, approved, scheduled, published); paid amplification (yes/no, budget); KPI target; result; notes.

### 4.3 Cadence sanity check
Total monthly posts per platform times production hours per post must fit the team's capacity with a 20% buffer. If it does not, cut channels or cadence before quality.

## 5. Paid social

- Map objective to campaign type: awareness (reach, video views), consideration (traffic, engagement, lead forms), conversion (sales, app installs), retention (custom audiences, catalogue).
- Creative drives most of the performance. Test 3 to 5 concepts with 2 to 3 variations each; kill losers within 3 to 5 days once past learning; refresh winners every 2 to 4 weeks to fight fatigue.
- Budget split starting point: 70% proven, 20% scaling promising, 10% experimental.
- Audience: broad targeting with strong creative often beats narrow interest stacks on Meta and TikTok; use first-party audiences for retention and exclusion; layer retargeting by intent (viewed 75% of video, visited product page, added to cart).
- Frequency: cap around 2 to 3 per week for prospecting; watch for rising CPM and falling CTR as fatigue signals.
- Measurement: agree the attribution model up front; supplement platform reporting with holdout tests, geo tests or MMM where spend justifies it.
- Boost organic winners only; never boost content that failed organically to rescue it.

## 6. Measurement

### 6.1 KPI tree
| Objective | Primary KPI | Supporting KPIs |
|---|---|---|
| Awareness | Reach (unique), share of voice | Impressions, video views, follower growth, brand search lift |
| Consideration | Engagement rate by reach | Saves, shares, comments, profile visits, watch time, link clicks |
| Conversion | Conversions and revenue attributed | CTR, CPC, CPA, ROAS, leads, promo code redemptions |
| Loyalty and advocacy | Repeat engagement rate, UGC volume | Community size and activity, response time, sentiment, retention |

### 6.2 Formulas
- Engagement rate by reach = (likes + comments + saves + shares) / reach.
- Save rate = saves / reach. Share rate = shares / reach.
- Hook rate = 3-second views / impressions. Completion rate = full views / views.
- CTR = clicks / impressions. CPM = spend / impressions × 1000. CPA = spend / conversions. ROAS = attributed revenue / spend.
- Share of voice = brand mentions / total category mentions.

### 6.3 Reporting rhythm
Weekly pulse (what ran, what worked, what changes next week); monthly review against targets with insights and decisions; quarterly strategy review against the objective cascade. Every report answers: what happened, why, so what, what next.

### 6.4 Test-and-learn
One variable per test; define the success metric and minimum sample before launch; run at least two weeks or until significance; write down the learning and fold it into the playbook.

## 7. Community management

- Response SLAs: comments and DMs within 2 hours in business hours, within 24 hours otherwise; service issues faster than marketing praise.
- Tone: human, first name, brand voice; move complaints to DM after one public acknowledgement; never argue in public.
- Proactive engagement: spend time daily commenting on audience, creator and partner content; this builds reach and relationships.
- Moderation: publish community guidelines; hide or remove hate, spam and doxxing; document decisions.
- Crisis protocol: pause scheduled content; assess severity and facts within the hour; one spokesperson and one approved message; respond publicly once, then take it offline; monitor sentiment; debrief within a week.

## 8. Deliverable standards

### 8.1 Deck structure
1. Executive summary: the answer, three to five supporting points, the ask.
2. Situation and insight: where the client is, what the audience and market tell us, the core problem or opportunity.
3. Strategy: objective cascade, audience segments, channel roles, content pillars and mix, creative principles.
4. Plan: calendar overview, tentpoles, series, production and operating model.
5. Measurement: KPI tree, targets, reporting rhythm, test plan.
6. Roadmap: phased, with quick wins, dependencies and decisions needed.
7. Appendix: audit detail, benchmarks, sources, specs.

### 8.2 Slide anatomy
Action title that states the takeaway in one sentence. One idea per slide. Evidence beneath the title (chart, table, example, quote). Source or assumption in the footer. Consistent template, brand colours where the client's brand applies, generous white space, minimum 12pt text. Charts follow the dataviz skill.

### 8.3 Executive summary test
A reader who sees only the executive summary should know the recommendation, why, what it costs, what it delivers, and what decision is needed.

### 8.4 QA checklist
- Every number and date is correct and sourced or labelled as an assumption.
- Every recommendation traces to the objective cascade.
- Inside brand guidelines and voice; nothing off-limits.
- Platform specifics verified and date-stamped.
- No spelling, grammar or formatting errors; consistent terminology.
- No filler slides; every slide passes the "so what" test.
- Story reads coherently from the executive summary alone.
- Deliverable format matches the ask (deck, PDF, spreadsheet) and opens cleanly.
- Benchmarked against best-in-class work; raised where it fell short.

## 9. Brief question bank

Ask only the questions whose answers would change the work.

**Client and goal**: Who is the client? What business goal must social contribute to, by when? Is there a revenue or volume target?
**Audience**: Who are we reaching? What do we know from analytics, CRM, research? Where are they in the journey?
**Channels**: Which channels are in scope? Which formats? Any channels to stop or launch?
**Time frame and moments**: Start and end dates? Launches, sales events, seasonal or cultural dates, events, PR?
**Resourcing and budget**: Sustainable cadence? Who produces creative? Existing assets or shoots? Paid, creator or tooling budget?
**Brand**: Brand guidelines, tone of voice, visual identity? Off-limits topics, claims, humour, competitor mentions, regulated language?
**What exists**: Current strategy, pillars, calendars, past performance, what worked and did not? Where does it live?
**Measurement**: How will success be judged? Baselines and targets? Attribution approach?
**Deliverable**: Who receives it and will it be presented or read? Deck, PDF, spreadsheet, or all? Deadline?
